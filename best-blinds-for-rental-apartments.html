Rental Apartment Guide

What Are the Best Blinds for Rental Apartments?

Discover renter-friendly blinds, tension rods, no-drill shades, removable films and reversible installation ideas without unnecessary permanent damage.

Published & updated 26 July 2026Reviewed by installation team1,300 words
Call 9870167217 WhatsApp Advice

Direct answer

The best rental-apartment blinds are lightweight, removable and easy to return to the original condition. Tension-mounted shades, compatible clip-on systems, removable paper shades and curtain rods can suit short stays. For longer rentals, ask the landlord for written permission to install a proper blind and agree how holes will be repaired.

Key takeaways

  • Check the lease and obtain written permission before drilling.
  • No-drill solutions must still be suitable for the frame and blind weight.
  • Temporary products trade durability and fit for reversibility.
  • Keep original hardware and photograph the window before installation.

Start with the lease, not the product

Rental rules differ. Some landlords allow a few neat holes; others prohibit drilling into tiles, aluminium frames or exterior walls. Ask in writing before removing existing window treatments or attaching anything permanent.

Photograph the opening and existing hardware before work starts. Store original brackets, screws, curtains and blinds in a labelled box so the apartment can be restored when you leave.

Best temporary blind options

Tension-mounted shades

These sit within a suitable recess without screws. They work only when the recess is strong, square and compatible with the product’s width and weight.

Clip-on or clamp systems

Some products attach to compatible uPVC or metal frames. Confirm that the clamp will not damage seals or obstruct the window.

Removable paper or fabric shades

Useful as a low-cost short-term solution. Adhesive quality, humidity and surface finish affect removal.

Tension curtain rods

A lightweight curtain can provide privacy without drilling when the recess supports the rod. Do not overload it.

Removable privacy film

Frosted static-cling film can provide privacy while admitting light. Reflective one-way film is not dependable for night privacy.

When a proper installed blind is better

Temporary products may sag, leave gaps or fail on wide windows. For a multi-year tenancy, a measured roller, Roman, bamboo or PVC blind can improve comfort and appearance enough to justify landlord approval.

Offer a simple proposal: product type, colour, fixing points, installer, who pays, and whether the blind stays with the property. A neutral, professionally installed blind may be seen as an improvement rather than damage.

Avoid these renter mistakes

  • Using strong adhesive on fresh paint, veneer or delicate laminate.
  • Clamping to a frame without checking seals and opening movement.
  • Hanging a heavy blind from a tension rod.
  • Drilling into tile, metal or a concealed-services area without permission.
  • Discarding the landlord’s original hardware.
  • Assuming a “no-drill” product is safe for children, pets or windy balconies.

A practical approval message

Example: “I would like to install a neutral custom roller blind on the living-room window. A professional will use two small bracket locations above the recess. I will keep the existing curtain and can either leave the blind for the property or have the holes repaired at the end of the tenancy. Please confirm approval in writing.”

No-drill does not mean no risk

A tension rod can fall when it is overloaded or fitted to a weak, tapered or polished recess. Adhesive can pull paint from a wall or leave residue on laminate. A clamp can compress a window seal or prevent the sash from closing. Before using any no-drill product, check the frame material, weight limit and opening movement.

Temporary paper shades are useful for immediate privacy but can look less finished and may not tolerate humidity. Static-cling film is reversible on suitable clean glass, yet it changes the view and does not solve heat or darkness in every room. The safest temporary product is the one matched to the surface rather than the one advertised with the strongest claim.

Permission, documentation and restoration

Send the landlord a written proposal with a product photograph, colour, mounting location and number of holes. Explain whether the blind will remain with the property or be removed. Ask who is responsible for patching and repainting. Written approval protects both sides better than a casual conversation.

Before installation, photograph the frame, wall and existing treatment. Label and store every removed screw, bracket and curtain. When leaving, use suitable filler and paint rather than hiding holes with tape or furniture. A professional restoration is often cheaper than a deposit dispute.

Long-stay principle: a neat, neutral custom blind approved by the landlord can be better value than replacing several weak temporary products over a multi-year tenancy.

Safety, children, pets and everyday use

A temporary installation still needs to be safe. Loose cords can be hazardous around young children and pets, while a weak tension rod can fall when a curtain is pulled. Keep beds, cots, sofas and climbable furniture away from control cords. Use suitable tension devices or choose cordless products where available.

Pets may push through a blind near a balcony door, chew cords or damage lightweight paper shades. Leave a practical access route and avoid placing a fragile temporary treatment where it will be handled many times a day. A cheap product that repeatedly falls or obstructs the door is not genuinely renter-friendly.

Balcony installations deserve particular caution. A no-drill indoor product should not be assumed safe in outdoor wind. Never suspend a heavy bamboo or PVC blind from a light tension rod. Outdoor panels need appropriate support, a safe raised position during strong weather and landlord approval for the fixing method.

Check every temporary product after the first few days and then periodically. Look for slipping rods, peeling adhesive, marks on the frame and loosening clamps. Remove the item before it fails and damages the property. Reversibility matters, but stability and safe daily operation come first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I install blinds without drilling?

Some tension, clip-on, adhesive or static-cling products do not need drilling, but compatibility and weight limits must be checked.

Can a landlord charge for blind installation holes?

Lease terms and local rules vary. Obtain written permission and agree whether holes must be repaired when the tenancy ends.

Are bamboo blinds suitable for rentals?

They can be, especially for a covered balcony or natural interior look, but proper mounting may require landlord approval.

What is the easiest temporary privacy option?

Static-cling frosted film or a lightweight tension-mounted curtain can be simple, depending on the window and required privacy.

Need a custom recommendation?

Discuss your window, balcony or outdoor opening

Share photographs and approximate measurements. Final production should use confirmed measurements.
