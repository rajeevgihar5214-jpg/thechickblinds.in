Cost & Budget Guide

How Much Do Quality Blinds Actually Cost?

Plan a realistic blind budget by understanding material, size, lining, hardware, motorisation, measurement and installation costs in 2026.

Published & updated 26 July 2026Reviewed by installation team1,411 words

Direct answer

Quality blind cost depends on measured area, material, mechanism, lining, hardware, custom shape and installation. In Delhi market listings reviewed in July 2026, basic bamboo chick products appeared around ₹30–₹100 per sq ft and one roller-blind listing was around ₹150 per sq ft, but retail custom projects can cost more after size, finishing, hardware, minimum order, GST, transport and fitting are included.

Key takeaways

  • Compare complete installed cost, not only a headline per-square-foot rate.
  • Minimum billing can make a small window cost more per square foot.
  • Custom Roman, zebra, premium roller and motorised systems usually cost more than basic manual products.
  • Request a written specification so quotations are genuinely comparable.

Why blind prices vary so widely

Two blinds covering the same window can have very different prices because they use different fabric, bamboo grade, coating, tube, brackets, control chain, lining and finishing. One may be a ready-made product; the other may be measured, manufactured and fitted for a specific opening.

A price shown online may also be wholesale, subject to a minimum order or exclude fitting. Treat it as a market signal, not a final quotation for your home.

A practical 2026 market reference

Delhi supplier and marketplace listings checked in July 2026 showed examples of bamboo chick products at approximately ₹30–₹100 per square foot. A separate Delhi vendor displayed a roller-blind example around ₹150 per square foot. These examples vary in minimum quantity, finish and specification and should not be treated as The Chick Blinds’ fixed rates.

Budget carefully: The final installed amount may include minimum billing, edging, coating, lining, upgraded hardware, brackets, transport, GST, difficult access and installation.

The basic pricing formula

Estimated project costMeasured billable area × product rate + upgrades + installation + travel/taxes

Some suppliers apply a minimum billable width, height or total order value. This means a very small window may not be priced only on its exact glass area. Ask how billable area is calculated before comparing quotes.

Budget tiers: what you are really choosing

Value-focused

Simple manual operation, standard finish, practical material and straightforward installation. Best when function matters more than decorative detailing.

Mid-range custom

Made-to-measure dimensions, improved fabric or weave, stronger hardware, better privacy options and coordinated finishes.

Premium

Specialist materials, Roman construction, decorative borders, cassette systems, large dimensions, motorisation or integration with a smart-home routine.

Price alone cannot identify quality. Check stitching, tube strength, bracket gauge, chain or cord operation, edge finishing, alignment and after-sales terms.

How to compare blind quotations correctly

  1. Ask for the exact product name and material specification.
  2. Confirm finished width, drop and number of panels.
  3. Ask whether the price includes brackets, fitting and adjustment.
  4. Check whether GST, transport and minimum billing are included.
  5. Confirm lining, edging, colour, coating and control side.
  6. Ask what is covered if the blind tracks badly or hardware fails.

Example budget scenarios

Consider three windows of the same nominal size. A basic manual roller using a standard fabric may have a modest material cost and simple brackets. A Roman blind adds more fabric, lining, stitching, rods and labour. A motorised roller adds a motor, stronger tube, control equipment and installation time. The area is similar, but the systems are not comparable.

A small order can also carry a minimum manufacturing or visit charge. Conversely, a larger multi-room order may use the same site visit and reduce the average installation cost per blind. Wide balconies can become expensive if they need several balanced panels, outdoor hardware and difficult access.

Budget with a contingency for the items that are often decided after inspection: stronger supports, privacy lining, decorative edging, a weather-resistant upgrade or removal of an existing track.

Signs that a low quotation may be false economy

  • The material is described only as “premium” without a sample or specification.
  • The quotation does not state finished dimensions or number of panels.
  • Installation, brackets, GST and transport are left unclear.
  • A very wide blind is proposed without discussing tube strength or weight.
  • An outdoor product is sold without addressing rain, wind or corrosion.
  • No one confirms the control side, mounting position or side gaps.
  • Warranty language is broad but excludes the mechanism or fitting.

A higher quote is not automatically better, but a complete quote is easier to judge. Ask each supplier to price the same specification. Comparing one lined custom Roman blind with an unlined standard roller tells you little about value.

How to prepare a quotation brief

Send every supplier the same short brief: room name, width, height, mounting position, photographs, preferred product, privacy requirement, exposure, colour family and whether fitting is required. Ask for the complete installed amount and the assumptions used.

Example brief: “Covered west-facing balcony, three openings, approximate total width 4.8 m, strong afternoon sun, moderate rain from the side, needs privacy and daily door access. Please quote suitable bamboo and PVC options, number of panels, hardware, measurement, installation, GST and expected lead time.”

Think about lifetime cost, not only purchase price

A blind that is cheap to buy but difficult to clean, impossible to repair or unsuitable for the exposure can cost more over time. Ask whether cords, chains, carriers, brackets or motors can be serviced separately. Replaceable components may extend useful life without replacing the complete blind.

Maintenance also has a cost. Venetian and vertical slats need regular dusting. Roman fabrics can require specialist cleaning. Natural bamboo needs protection from repeated moisture, while exposed PVC should be checked for hardware wear and wind damage. Choose a material that fits the amount of maintenance you are realistically willing to do.

Include the expected period of use in the budget. A landlord-approved neutral blind for a long tenancy may offer better value than several temporary replacements. For a commercial space, reliable daily operation and fast repair support may matter more than a small initial saving.

Good value is the combination of suitable material, accurate sizing, sound hardware, correct fitting and clear after-sales terms. The cheapest rate per square foot cannot show all of those factors.

Frequently asked questions

Are blinds usually priced per square foot?

Many custom blinds are quoted using billable area, but suppliers may also apply minimum sizes, minimum order values and separate charges for hardware or installation.

Why are motorised blinds more expensive?

They add a motor, power or battery system, control equipment and setup. Large motorised blinds may also require stronger tubes and brackets.

Does a blind quotation include installation?

Not always. Ask whether measurement, brackets, fitting, transport, GST and final adjustment are included.
